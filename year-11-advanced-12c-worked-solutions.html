Exercise 12C — Worked solutions

Year 11 Mathematics Advanced · Sample space graphs and tree diagrams
CambridgeMATHS NSW Advanced Year 11 · printed pp. 443–444 · finals checked against the answers booklet

How to use this sheet: draw the sample space first (tree or graph). Then count. Then write P = (how many you want) / (how many there are). Every outcome on a counting tree is equally likely.

Foundation Question 1 · printed p. 443

A fair coin is tossed twice.

a Use a tree diagram to list the four possible outcomes.

b i two heads   ii a head and a tail   iii a head on the first toss and a tail on the second

There are 4 equally likely outcomes.

i Only HH is two heads. Answer: 1/4

ii A head and a tail means HT or TH (order can swap). That is 2 outcomes. Answer: 2/4 = 1/2

iii This time the order is fixed: first H, then T. Only HT. Answer: 1/4

Foundation Question 2 · printed p. 443

A coin is tossed and a die is thrown.

a Use a tree diagram to list all 12 possible outcomes.

b Find the probability of:

|S| = 12.

i Head and even: H2, H4, H6 — three outcomes. Answer: 3/12 = 1/4

ii Tail and greater than 4: T5, T6. Answer: 2/12 = 1/6

iii Tail and less than 4: T1, T2, T3. Answer: 3/12 = 1/4

iv Head and prime. Primes on a die are 2, 3 and 5 (1 is not prime). So H2, H3, H5. Answer: 3/12 = 1/4

Foundation Question 3 · printed p. 443

Two tiles are chosen at random, one after the other, from three Scrabble tiles T, O and E, and placed in order on the table. (No replacement.)

a List the six possible outcomes in the sample space.

First tile: 3 choices. Second tile: 2 left. So 3 × 2 = 6 ordered pairs.

Answer: TO, OT, OE, EO, ET, TE

b Find the probability of choosing:

i Two vowels: OT, OE, EO. (T is the only consonant.) Three outcomes. Answer: 3/6 = 1/3

ii A consonant then a vowel: T then O, or T then E → TO, TE. Answer: 2/6 = 1/3

iii A T: T can be first (TO, TE) or second (OT, ET). Four outcomes. Answer: 4/6 = 2/3

Foundation Question 4 · printed p. 443

From Anna, Bill, Charlie and David, two are chosen at random, one after the other, for the SRC. Order matters (first then second).

a List the 12 possible outcomes.

4 choices for first, then 3 left for second. 4 × 3 = 12.

Answer: AB, BA, AC, CA, AD, DA, BC, CB, BD, DB, CD, DC

b Find the probability that:

i Bill and David are chosen: BD and DB. Answer: 2/12 = 1/6

ii Anna is chosen: she is first (AB, AC, AD) or second (BA, CA, DA). Six outcomes. Answer: 6/12 = 1/2

iii Charlie is chosen but Bill is not: the pair is from {A, C, D} and must include C. That is CA, AC, CD, DC. (CB and BC include Bill.) Answer: 4/12 = 1/3

iv Neither Anna nor David: only Bill and Charlie. BC and CB. Answer: 2/12 = 1/6

v Bill is chosen first: BA, BC, BD. Answer: 3/12 = 1/4

vi Charlie is not chosen second: Charlie second would be AC, BC, DC (3 outcomes), so not those: 12 − 3 = 9. Answer: 9/12 = 3/4

Foundation Question 5 · printed p. 443

From the digits 2, 3, 8 and 9, a two-digit number is formed with no digit repeated.

a Use a tree diagram to list the possible outcomes.

b If the number was formed at random, find the probability that it is:

|S| = 12.

i The number 82: only one outcome. Answer: 1/12

ii Greater than 39: 82, 83, 89, 92, 93, 98. (32 and 23 are smaller; 38, 39 are not greater than 39.) Six outcomes. Answer: 6/12 = 1/2

iii Even: the last digit must be 2 or 8. Ending in 2: 32, 82, 92. Ending in 8: 28, 38, 98. Six outcomes. Answer: 6/12 = 1/2

iv A multiple of 3: a number is a multiple of 3 if its digits add to a multiple of 3. Digit-sum 2+3=5, 2+8=10, 2+9=11, 3+8=11, 3+9=12, 8+9=17. Only 3 and 9 give sum 12. The two numbers are 39 and 93. Answer: 2/12 = 1/6

v Ending in 2: 32, 82, 92. Answer: 3/12 = 1/4

vi A perfect square: possible two-digit squares from these digits would have to be 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100… none of 23, 32, 28, 82, 29, 92, 38, 83, 39, 93, 89, 98 is a square. Answer: 0

Foundation Question 6 · printed p. 444

A captain and vice-captain are chosen from Amanda, Belinda, Carol, Dianne and Emma. Order is important.

a Use a tree diagram to list the possible pairings.

5 choices for captain, then 4 left for vice-captain. 5 × 4 = 20 pairings. The captain is written first.

Answer: AB, AC, AD, AE, BC, BD, BE, CD, CE, DE, BA, CA, DA, EA, CB, DB, EB, DC, EC, ED

Tree idea: Start splits into A, B, C, D, E (captain). From A the vice-captain branches are B, C, D, E. The same pattern repeats for each captain.

b If the choices were made at random:

|S| = 20.

i Carol captain and Emma vice-captain: only CE. Answer: 1/20

ii Belinda is captain or vice-captain: captain B → BA, BC, BD, BE (4); vice-captain B → AB, CB, DB, EB (4). Total 8. Answer: 8/20 = 2/5

iii Amanda is not selected: choose captain and vice from {B, C, D, E}. 4 × 3 = 12. Answer: 12/20 = 3/5

iv Emma is vice-captain: AE, BE, CE, DE. Answer: 4/20 = 1/5

Development Question 7 · printed p. 444

A coin is tossed three times. Draw a tree, then find each probability.

The eight equally likely outcomes are HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT, THH, THT, TTH, TTT.

a Three heads: only HHH. Answer: 1/8

b A head and two tails: HTT, THT, TTH. Answer: 3/8

c At least two tails: two tails or three tails → HTT, THT, TTH, TTT. Answer: 4/8 = 1/2

d At most one head: 0 heads or 1 head → TTT, HTT, THT, TTH. Answer: 4/8 = 1/2

e More heads than tails: that means 2 or 3 heads → HHT, HTH, THH, HHH. Answer: 4/8 = 1/2

f A head on the second toss: the middle letter is H → HHH, HHT, THH, THT. Answer: 4/8 = 1/2

Development Question 8 · printed p. 444

A green die and a red die are thrown simultaneously. List the 36 outcomes on a two-dimensional graph, then find each probability. Label GxRy for green x and red y.

|S| = 36 equally likely dots.

a Three on the green die: the whole G3 column, 6 dots. Answer: 6/36 = 1/6

b Four on the red die: the whole R4 row, 6 dots. Answer: 6/36 = 1/6

c Double five: only G5R5. Answer: 1/36

d Total seven: G1R6, G2R5, G3R4, G4R3, G5R2, G6R1. Six dots. Answer: 6/36 = 1/6

e Total greater than nine: sums 10, 11, 12 → G4R6, G5R5, G5R6, G6R4, G6R5, G6R6. Six dots. Answer: 6/36 = 1/6

f Even on both: green 2,4,6 and red 2,4,6. That is 3 × 3 = 9 dots. Answer: 9/36 = 1/4

g At least one two: G2 row-of-green (6 dots) plus red 2 with green not 2 (5 more). 11 dots. Answer: 11/36

h Neither a one nor a four: each die may only show 2, 3, 5 or 6. 4 × 4 = 16 dots. Answer: 16/36 = 4/9

i A five and a number greater than three: (green 5 and red 4,5,6) or (red 5 and green 4,5,6), but G5R5 counted once. 3 + 3 − 1 = 5 dots. Answer: 5/36

j The same number on both dice: the six doubles G1R1 … G6R6. Answer: 6/36 = 1/6

Development Question 9 · printed p. 444

Births of boys and girls are equally likely.

a Family of two children

Tree: BB, BG, GB, GG. Four equally likely outcomes.

i Two girls: GG. Answer: 1/4

ii No girls: BB. Answer: 1/4

iii One boy and one girl: BG and GB. Answer: 2/4 = 1/2

b Family of three children

Same tree as three coins, with B for H and G for T (or the other way). Eight outcomes: BBB, BBG, BGB, BGG, GBB, GBG, GGB, GGG.

i Three boys: BBB. Answer: 1/8

ii Two girls and one boy: BGG, GBG, GGB. Answer: 3/8

iii More boys than girls: 2 boys or 3 boys → BBG, BGB, GBB, BBB. Answer: 4/8 = 1/2

Development Question 10 · printed p. 444

A coin is tossed four times. |S| = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16 equally likely outcomes.

The 16 outcomes are all strings of four letters from {H, T}: HHHH, HHHT, HHTH, HHTT, HTHH, HTHT, HTTH, HTTT, THHH, THHT, THTH, THTT, TTHH, TTHT, TTTH, TTTT.

a Four heads: only HHHH. Answer: 1/16

b Exactly three tails: exactly one H. The H can sit in 4 places: HTTT, THTT, TTHT, TTTH. Answer: 4/16 = 1/4

c At least two heads: easier with the complement. Not this event means 0 heads or 1 head: TTTT plus the four “exactly one H” strings → 5 outcomes. So 16 − 5 = 11. Answer: 11/16

d At most one head: 0 or 1 head — the same 5 outcomes as above. Answer: 5/16

e Two heads and two tails: choose 2 positions out of 4 for the heads. The six strings are HHTT, HTHT, HTTH, THHT, THTH, TTHH. Answer: 6/16 = 3/8

f More tails than heads: 3 tails (4 outcomes) or 4 tails (1 outcome). Answer: 5/16

Development Question 11 · printed p. 444

A hand has ten, jack, queen, king and ace. Two cards are drawn in succession without replacement.

First card: 5 choices. Second: 4 left. |S| = 20 equally likely ordered pairs.

a The ace is drawn (first or second). Complement: both cards from {10, J, Q, K}. That is 4 × 3 = 12 pairs with no ace, so 20 − 12 = 8 have the ace. Answer: 8/20 = 2/5

b The king is not drawn: both from {10, J, Q, A}. 4 × 3 = 12. Answer: 12/20 = 3/5

c The queen is the second card: first can be any of the other four, then Q. 4 × 1 = 4. Answer: 4/20 = 1/5

Challenge Question 12 · printed p. 444

Three-digit numbers from the digits 2, 5, 7 and 8, without repetition.

a Tree diagram. How many outcomes?

First digit: 4 choices. Second: 3 left. Third: 2 left. 4 × 3 × 2 = 24.

Answer: 24 outcomes

Full list: 257, 258, 275, 278, 285, 287, 527, 528, 572, 578, 582, 587, 725, 728, 752, 758, 782, 785, 825, 827, 852, 857, 872, 875.

b Probability the number is:

|S| = 24.

i Greater than 528. Count from the list: 572, 578, 582, 587, 725, 728, 752, 758, 782, 785, 825, 827, 852, 857, 872, 875. That is 16 numbers. (528 itself is not greater; 527 and 258 and so on are smaller.) Answer: 16/24 = 2/3

ii Divisible by 3: digit-sum must be a multiple of 3. The only triple whose digits add to a multiple of 3 is 2, 5, 8 (sum 15). The six permutations are 258, 285, 528, 582, 825, 852. Answer: 6/24 = 1/4

iii Divisible by 13: from the list, 572 = 13 × 44 and 728 = 13 × 56. Two numbers. Answer: 2/24 = 1/12

iv Prime: even numbers and those ending in 5 are not prime. Left to test: 257, 527, 275 already out, 287 = 7 × 41, 527 = 17 × 31, 577 not in the list. The primes on the list are 257, 587, 827 and 857. Four numbers. Answer: 4/24 = 1/6

Challenge Question 13 · printed p. 444

A coin is tossed n times, where n > 1.

Each toss has 2 equally likely results, so |S| = 2n.

a n heads: only one string, all heads. Answer: 1 / 2n

b At least one head and at least one tail means “not all heads and not all tails”. P(all heads) = 1/2n and P(all tails) = 1/2n, and those two outcomes do not overlap. So P = 1 − 2/2n = 1 − 21−n.

Answer: 1 − 21−n
